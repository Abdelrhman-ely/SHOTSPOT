The problem

At the south pole, a few meters decide the mission.

Everyone wants to land there for the water ice. Three things make it hard:

The Sun barely rises

It skims a few degrees above the horizon. A distant ridge can hide it for days, and no sunlight means no power.

Earth slips below the horizon

Earth sits low too. When terrain hides it, the team can't talk to the lander.

Landers miss their mark

Landers miss their aim by hundreds of meters, as Athena did in 2025. A perfect spot is useless if its neighbors are not.

Aim here, drift up to X meters: how many spots still work, and where should you aim instead?

SHOTSPOT answers that with real NASA terrain and ephemeris data.
